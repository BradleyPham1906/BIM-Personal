Sheets

  • Layouts: LAYOUT adds a sheet layout with viewports. TITLEBLOCK edits the title block.
  • Model and paper space: MSPACE and PSPACE switch between them.
  • Presenting: the Presentation tab on the left rail lists the pages of the set, with their thumbnails. From there you can present them full screen or print the set.
  • Boards in the set: + Board adds a board of the site analysis as a page: Climate and risk, Zoning and yield, or Access and people. Analyze's Show in Presentation does the same.
    • A board's page opens in the main view, with the panels beside it, and lays out by the room it has. Esc gives the drawing back.
    • Drag a board to move it among the sheets; the sheets keep their own order, as the layout tabs show it. Right-click it to present from it, print it alone, move it or remove it from the set. Its data stays in Analyze.
    • Present shows it as on screen; the wheel scrolls it to its end, then goes on to the next page. Print set prints it on A3 landscape pages of its own, in its place in the set.
  • Printing one sheet: PLOT.

Arranging a board

A board's Edit, on its bar, arranges its figures, as a sheet's viewports are arranged:

  • Drag a figure by its handle or its body; size it by its corner. Snap, Grid and Ortho are the drawing's own (F3, F9, F8) and govern it: Snap catches an edge on another figure's or the board's, Grid puts the edges on the board's 12 columns and on rows of the same step, Ortho keeps a drag straight across or straight down.
  • The arrow keys move the figure in focus; Shift and the arrow keys size it; Delete removes it.
  • A figure's menu (its button, or a right-click): Options (its title; the chart, its numbers as a table, or both; UTCI's condition), Fit the height to its content, Duplicate, earlier or later in reading order, Remove.
  • Add a figure lists every analysis the project has, from any board, each with its preview; it goes below the others at its own width.
  • Reset gives one of the three boards its own figures in their own places again.

Each change is one undo step and is kept with the project. A board not yet arranged keeps its own layout. Once it is arranged, figures of its own that come later, with new data, wait below the others until placed. Present, Print set and the thumbnail show a board as arranged. On a phone its figures are in reading order, and each one's menu moves it earlier or later.

+ Board, then New board, makes a board of your own: name it in Edit, then add any analysis to it. A board's menu in Presentation duplicates it as a new board, and deletes a board of your own (Ctrl+Z brings it back).

Designing a board

A board is paper space, as an AutoCAD layout is, and what you draw on it is on the project's own layers: the same layers as the model, in the same Layers tab and Layer Properties Manager.

  • Draw, on the board's Edit bar: a rectangle, an ellipse, a line, an arrow, a polygon, a star, text, an image, or a map frame with its legend, north arrow and scale bar. It goes on the current layer.
  • Selecting: click an object; Shift or Ctrl adds. Its properties are in the Properties panel, and its row is under its layer in Layers, where a click selects it too. Locked and frozen layers' objects are not picked; a layer that is off hides them; a layer set not to plot leaves them off paper.
  • What is drawn over what: the Layers list, read from the bottom up: a layer higher in the list is drawn over those below. Move a layer with the Up and Down buttons, or drop it on the top or bottom of another layer's row (on its middle, it becomes a sub-layer). Within a layer, Draw order in Properties: To front, Forward, Backward, To back (Ctrl+Shift+], Ctrl+], Ctrl+[, Ctrl+Shift+[).
  • Transparency and blend modes: Blend is a layer property beside Transparency in the Layer Properties Manager: the sixteen modes (Multiply, Screen, Overlay and the rest). An object takes its layer's, ByLayer, unless it has its own in Properties. They are drawn in the Presentation appearance (Pres in the status bar) and in Present; the Technical appearance draws every object plain.
  • Masks: an object set as a mask (Alpha, Vector or Luminance) clips the objects of its own layer drawn above it, up to that layer's next mask.
  • Arranging: Properties' Arrange aligns the selection (one object aligns to the page), distributes three or more with equal gaps between the outermost two, or tidies them to their most common gap. While you drag, Snap (F3) catches other objects' edges, centres and equal gaps.
  • Stacks: a layer's objects on a board can be a Stack: a row, a column or rows that wrap, with a gap (or Auto), padding and alignment. Each object is Fixed, fills the width or the height, or is out of the stack. Drag an object to another place in its stack. On a phone a row is shown as a column.
  • Page: the first drawing on a board makes it a page, as a Figma frame or a Canva design has a size: the smallest standard page that holds what is on it (ISO A, or ANSI in an imperial project). Page changes it: a size from the standards (ISO A0 to A4, ANSI A to E, Architectural A to E1), landscape or portrait, with an ISO 5457 frame and ISO 7200 title block or a US title strip. A board with a page is that page on screen, in Present and on paper, printed at its own size. The title block takes the project's fields (Project, Client, Drawn by, Checked by); its number and date of issue are its own, in Properties. No page lets the board flow again; it then prints in A3 strips, which may cut a figure in two.
  • Map frames: a plan, an elevation or a saved view at a scale, panned; Fit shows it whole. The site's shown data layers and the basemap are drawn in it, and its corner credits their sources, always. The legend lists the shown data and result layers; the north arrow points to the site's true north (or plan north), plus an offset you type for magnetic declination; the scale bar picks a round length for the frame.
  • Images are kept once in the browser and travel inside the project file (4 MB each at most).

Live text on a board

Text on a board can follow the project, so a board says what the model says.

  • Field, in Draw: a value of the project put into a text. Pick it from the menu: the project's name, the client, who drew and checked it, today's date, the board's name and number, the gross floor area, the room area, the site area, the floor area the zoning allows, the cut and fill volumes. In the text it is written {{key}}, for example Site area: {{site.area}}; {{site.area|2}} gives two decimals. With a text selected the field goes at its end; with none, a new text is made.
  • Number, in Draw: one number with its unit, in large type, for a figure that stands alone: the gross floor area, a level's gross area, the site area, cut or fill. Its decimals are in Properties.
  • It is drawn from the project each time the board is, on screen, in Present and on paper. The value is never kept in the file, only what it is bound to.
  • Override: in Properties, the Shows row holds what the text shows. Type other words there and the text keeps them, marked with a blue corner tick. Reset goes back to the source. If the source changes after you typed, Properties says so: Reset takes the new value, Keep keeps yours. Making a live text bold, larger or another colour is not an override.
  • Unlink leaves what the text shows as plain text; it no longer follows the project.
  • Marks, in Edit only and never printed: an amber dot where the source has nothing to show yet or is out of date (cut and fill after a pad has changed: Run again in Properties grades again; a board never reruns an analysis by itself), a red square where the source was removed or a key is not a field. A deleted level says which level it was; nothing is bound to another by guess. On paper such a value is a dash, and Print says which live objects are not up to date.
  • A key that is not a field stays as typed. The project's display units (m, cm, mm and their decimals) are now kept with the project.
  • Snap, Grid and Ortho are the status bar's, one set for the model, sheets and boards (F3, F9, F8). On a sheet they apply when a viewport is moved or sized: Grid keeps whole millimetres, Ortho a straight move, Snap catches the paper's and the other viewports' edges and middles. Technical style and Presentation style, beside them, set how the drawing looks; Present, in the Presentation panel, is the slideshow.
  • Schedule, in Draw: a table of the model, Rooms or Areas by level, with a total row. In Properties: its title, the column it is sorted by, and Descending. It changes as the model does.
  • Indicator, in Draw: one card taken out of a site board's indicators (climate, zoning, access, people), placed alone. The site board must be worked out first, in Site analysis.
  • Result chart, in Draw: one result layer's chart and what it measured; for Sun hours, how much of the ground gets how many hours of sun. Two numbers join Number with it: the share of the ground with 6 hours of sun or more, and the roofs' solar yield in a year.
  • A result that the model has moved on from is marked (the amber dot), and Run again in Properties runs that analysis again; a board never does so by itself. A removed layer says which one it was.

New Sheet chooses its page from the same gallery. Every change is one undo step and is kept with the project.

SketchUp files

SKPIN (also IMPORTSKP or SKETCHUP) opens a .skp file from any SketchUp, including SketchUp 2021 and later. The file is read by openskp's reader (MIT), which is built into the page, so nothing is uploaded. The model comes in where it was drawn, in metres:

  • Faces. Each group or component, in each of its materials, is one locked solid. Faces drawn loose in the model are one solid per material, for example "Concrete faces". A face is brought in once, even when its back is painted a colour of its own.
  • Tags. The file has a layer of its own, named after the file. Each tag is a sub-layer of it, for example "site_kit - Walls", in the tag's colour. A tag that was off in SketchUp is off here. Untagged faces go on the file's layer. Opening the same file again uses the same layers.
  • Materials. Each solid takes its material's colour. A textured material takes the colour SketchUp keeps with the texture (the image's average, which SketchUp's Shaded style shows). The texture's file is named in Properties. An unpainted face is SketchUp's white.
  • Glass. A see-through material goes on a sub-layer of its tag at the material's transparency, for example "site_kit - Windows - 65% see-through". This app keeps transparency on layers.
  • Components. Each component that has a name is kept in Assets → Blocks as a block from SketchUp, with what is inside it. It is kept in its own frame, and its insertion point is the block's base point. Groups, and components left with SketchUp's own name (Component#1), stay in the model only. Opening the same file again replaces its blocks.
  • Properties → SketchUp shows where a selected object came from: the file and the SketchUp that saved it, its component or group (and what it is inside), its tag, and its material.
  • Undo. The whole file is one undo step. The blocks stay in the library, which is kept apart from any project.

The library's Import in Assets also takes a .skp file. It keeps the file's components as blocks and places nothing.

Limits: 250,000 triangles a file; a block of up to 6,000 triangles, and as many blocks as fit in the library's storage. Each limit says when it is reached. A file the reader cannot read is refused, with the reader's reason. Some very old files (SketchUp 3) are among these.

Versions and history

The project keeps its own history, element by element. It works like git, at the grain of a building element.

Commit. Properties → Project → History (or HISTORY) lists what has changed since the last version. Each element that was added, removed or changed is listed. A changed element is listed field by field, for example "moved by 1, 0, 2", "name: "Site" → "North Lot"", or "Level 1 added". Type a message and press Enter or Commit to keep the model as a version. COMMIT puts the cursor in the message.

What a version stores. Every object is an element, by its id. So are the project's other parts: levels, grids, layers, types, sheets, the title block, buildings, the site, classifications and saved views. Each element is stored once, by a hash of its content. A version therefore adds only the elements that changed, and identical content is kept once.

Looking back.

  • Changes, on a version, opens what that version changed.
  • Restore brings the model back as it was at that version. It is an edit like any other: Undo takes it back. The history itself is never rewritten. Commit afterwards to keep the restored model as the latest version.
  • Discard Changes goes back to the latest version.

An element's own Properties show its History: the versions that added, changed or removed it, and what changed each time.

The history is saved with the project, in the browser and in the .acad3d.json project file.

Design options: branches

A branch is a name at a version: one design option. Every project starts on main.

  • New branch. In History, type a name in New branch and press Enter or New Branch (or BRANCH). The branch starts at the latest version and you are put on it. Changes not yet committed come along. A name is letters, numbers, spaces, dots and dashes, up to 40.
  • Switch. Pick a branch in the Branch list. The model becomes that branch's latest version, and Undo takes the switch back. Commit or discard your changes first, so nothing is lost.
  • Commit moves only the branch you are on. The version list shows that branch's line: its own versions and the ones it shares, with each branch's name on its latest version.
  • Compare (or COMPAREBRANCHES) puts each branch's numbers side by side: objects, walls and their length, doors and windows, rooms and their area, gross area, levels, cut and fill. Rows that differ are in bold. The branch you are on is counted as the model is now, committed or not.
  • Delete removes the branch picked in the list beside Merge In. It asks once first; the branch you are on cannot be deleted.

Merging

Merge In (or MERGE) brings the picked branch's work into the one you are on. The app finds the version both branches share and compares each element three ways: as it was there, as it is here, and as it is on the other branch.

  • If this branch has nothing new, it simply moves up to the other branch.
  • If this branch already has everything, nothing happens.
  • Otherwise each element that only one side changed is taken from that side. Where both sides changed the same element in different fields (one moved a column, the other renamed it), the fields are put together.
  • A conflict is the same field changed both ways, or an element deleted on one side and changed on the other. Conflicts are listed in History with the two sides next to each other. Choose Keep (this branch's) or Take (the other's) for each, then Finish Merge. Cancel leaves the model as it was.

A finished merge is a version with two parents, so the next merge between the same branches starts from it. Branches are saved with the project; a project from before V151 opens with its history on main. On a phone or a tablet, HISTORY, BRANCH, MERGE and COMPAREBRANCHES open the Properties sheet at History.
