# Later: Survey control — horizontal control and raw total-station observations

## 1. Abstract
How can World BIM move from checking terrain elevations to defensible horizontal control without silently treating calculated coordinates as original field observations? Recommend a provenance-preserving observation ledger, explicit datum/units and coordinate conventions, a closed-or-connected traverse diagnostic, and review-before-commit. Bowditch adjustment is an optional transparent preliminary aid, not a substitute for weighted least squares or a surveyor's determination. Start with one documented, license-clean raw format, not speculative vendor-format support. The code audit and synthetic closure calculation below are real; raw import, independent validation, actual hardware, and field work were not executed. This is research for a later phase, not a certified survey or an approved implementation.

## 2. Questions and falsifiable hypotheses
- H1: Existing V138 verifies horizontal control. Rejected: its control branch compares named elevations, not horizontal coordinate residuals. P/high, pinned code.
- H2: Existing traverse arithmetic is already a control-network adjustment. Rejected: it accumulates bearings and distances and calculates a scalar misclosure and ratio, with no weighting, residual model or covariance. P/high, pinned code.
- H3: A good closure proves positional accuracy or absence of mistakes. Rejected: systematic errors and reference uncertainty can coexist with closure; ICSM explicitly distinguishes survey, positional and relative uncertainties. P/high within the cited guidance's domain.
- H4: One generic parser can safely infer every raw format, units and correction state. Unverified and unsafe; instrument formats and exporter conventions differ. No vendor format was independently read here.
- H5: Computed coordinates should overwrite the measured data. Rejected as a design recommendation: immutable inputs plus versioned transformations enable audit and reversal; no app-level implementation demonstrated.

## 4. Findings and implications
### Existing implementation
[P/high] `src/js/engine/survey/survey-arithmetic.js` exports `bimParseBearing`, `bimParseLegs`, `bimTraverse` and `bimLegsFromRing`. It expresses azimuth clockwise from north, maps project north to negative Z and rotates by true north, sums legs and computes perimeter/misclosure. The parser requires at least three legs when there are no earlier parsing errors: that is a parcel-ring convention, not a general open-control traverse. It does not establish grid-vs-true north, grid scale, datum, or field observation corrections. [Code](https://github.com/BradleyPham1906/BIM-Personal/blob/95b7aee4db8be1fc28c0fbe7befc7407fabd1dda/src/js/engine/survey/survey-arithmetic.js).

[P/high] `survey-check.js` tests elevation `z` for named control points against `0.02 m`; it computes terrain diagnostics and check-shot vertical RMSE, not a 2D control network. Its aggregate status starts `pass` and changes only for `warn`/`fail`; a `none` control or check-shot item is not itself a failure. Thus a terrain pass does not prove control was supplied or that its horizontal coordinates are accurate. Avoid reusing the label as a control-survey pass. [Code](https://github.com/BradleyPham1906/BIM-Personal/blob/95b7aee4db8be1fc28c0fbe7befc7407fabd1dda/src/js/engine/survey/survey-check.js); [V138 research](https://github.com/BradleyPham1906/BIM-Personal/blob/95b7aee4db8be1fc28c0fbe7befc7407fabd1dda/docs/research/research-survey-check.md).

### Field data and coordinate contract
[P/high] An instrument measures horizontal and zenith angles and distances; equipment, calibration, targeting, observation technique and environmental corrections affect uncertainty. ICSM's 2020 SP1 guidance recommends recording relevant conditions, face-left/right and backsight/foresight practice, and examining survey and relative uncertainty; the guide is Australian practice, not a universal US legal specification. [ICSM traverse guide §§2–4](https://www.landgate.wa.gov.au/siteassets/documents/location-data-and-services/surveying/guideline-for-conventional-traverse-surveys_v2.2.pdf).

[U/proposal] Store raw-file hash, exact bytes/encoding, import profile and version; source line/record and warnings; instrument/setup/backsight IDs, occupied/target IDs, horizontal and vertical angles and their conventions, slope distance, instrument/target heights, timestamp, face, repeated sets, correction settings and unknowns. Keep derived horizontal distance, azimuth, station coordinate and geometry in a separate immutable computation revision. Unknown is not zero. Require explicit international feet versus US survey feet, angular unit, azimuth zero/direction, prism constant and correction-applied flags; do not double-correct. Mark unsupported proprietary records and continuation records with exact locations; reject missing orientation rather than guessing.

A minimum project control record needs horizontal CRS/datum, epoch where applicable, grid versus ground scale and convergence, vertical datum, units, source and uncertainty/covariance where available, and local model origin/rotation. Tie app's X/-Z world plan to E/N through a documented invertible transform. Do calculations in double-precision local coordinates; preserve external full-precision coordinates and raw text. Cross-datum comparison is disabled until a known transformation, area of use and required grids are available. Geometry in the scene remains provisional until reviewed.

### Adjustment and evidence
[P/high] ICSM adjustment guidance specifies known correction models, redundancy, measurement weights, minimally constrained/local and global tests, and appropriately weighted datum propagation. Relative uncertainty needs the full variance-covariance terms, not merely two independent point standard deviations. The example shows small survey uncertainty can coexist with larger positional uncertainty because control uncertainty propagates. [Adjustment guide §§2–5](https://www.landgate.wa.gov.au/siteassets/documents/location-data-and-services/surveying/guideline-for-adjustment-and-evaluation-of-survey-control_v2.2.pdf); [traverse guide §5](https://www.landgate.wa.gov.au/siteassets/documents/location-data-and-services/surveying/guideline-for-conventional-traverse-surveys_v2.2.pdf).

[U/proposal] Distinguish closed loop from link to known end, and distinguish computed closure diagnostics from statutory or project acceptance. A one-ended open traverse has no independent positional closure and must display `not assessable`, not pass. A weighted network adjustment requires a complete observation and stochastic model, control constraints, rank and redundancy checks, unit consistency, residual and test diagnostics, covariance and reviewer signoff. A robust global pass cannot identify every blunder; never auto-remove a suspect measurement without an auditable operator decision.

## 5. Alternatives compared
Criteria: traceability, trust, engineering scope, fit with app.
| Option | Advantages | Limitations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coordinate-only import with XY control comparison | Small extension of V138, useful for already processed coordinates | Cannot validate raw measurement or reconstruct station corrections |
| Raw ledger + traverse diagnostics | Reproducible field provenance and early feedback; reuses existing azimuth math | Not a complete weighted network or proof of accuracy |
| Full weighted network adjustment | Can assess residuals and uncertainty with valid models | Much greater validation and professional review burden |
| External licensed survey adjustment then import | Leverages mature tools | External workflow and traceability/integration required |
Recommend the ledger and clear diagnostics, permitting an externally adjusted dataset, before considering a solver. Choose a solver only after access to reference datasets and independent survey review.

## 6. Reproducible calculation
Synthetic closed loop: azimuth/distance (0°,100 m), (90°,80 m), (180°,100 m), (270°,79.98 m). With ΔE=d sin az and ΔN=d cos az: ΔE=+0.020 m, ΔN≈0; length=359.98 m; linear misclosure=0.020 m; unrounded ratio≈1:17,999. `experiment.py` and `results.json` record the actual Python run. This checks elementary closure only—not raw parser, geodetic reduction, instrument, app, weighted adjustment or compliance. Zero-misclosure ratio is represented separately as exact/undefined, not a misleading finite denominator. Do not choose a pass threshold from this synthetic example.

## 7. Edge and acceptance matrix (proposed, not executed)
1. Bearings N/E/S/W, 0/90/180/270 and DMS rollover; reject seconds ≥60 and ambiguous angle order; cardinal direction signed E/N match reference.
2. Closed loop, known-end link, open traverse, repeated shots, missing backsight, wrong point ID, disconnected component: display appropriate residual or unassessable; no invented closure.
3. Meters, international feet and US survey feet; grid vs ground, known rotation, translation, epoch and datum mismatch: independent fixture coordinates and roundtrip within agreed tolerance, otherwise block.
4. Slope vs horizontal distance and zenith conventions, face reversals, instrument/target height, corrections already applied vs not: compare a published/vendor example; retain intermediate values.
5. Weights absent, singular/rank deficient network, no redundancy, outlier, contradictory control, unknown covariance: no accuracy certificate; preserve observations and explanations.
6. Text encodings, bad line, truncated file, duplicate setup/point, large file and malformed numerics: report all rejected records; import atomically or visibly stage partial data; no silent overwrite.
7. Undo, save/reopen, recompute, exported audit package, edit controls and source revocation: preserve hashes, input revision, user choices, original data and old outputs.
8. Compare at least two independent commercially/reference adjusted field datasets and instrument exports, and perform licensed surveyor review before trusting results.
