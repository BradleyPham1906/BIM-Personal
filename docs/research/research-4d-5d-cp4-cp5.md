# CP4 procurement and CP5 drawing intake — technical research

2026-11-13. Draft research, not implementation approval, an estimate, a schedule, procurement advice, a compliance judgment or proof of an operational system. CP4 and CP5 are internal proposals, not new roadmap phases. Single author, no independent expert review. Source code and existing project plan inspected as text at main `95b7aee4db8be1fc28c0fbe7befc7407fabd1dda`; no app, supplier, AI service or real drawing was tested. A self-contained synthetic arithmetic fixture was run separately. This note supplements CP0–CP3 [1] and the existing planning task [2].

## Research questions and conclusions

1. Can a package's material, cost, activity and decision dates be traced without duplicating estimate value? Recommend allocation links to a versioned estimate snapshot, explicit unallocated amounts and package eligibility; never create duplicate independent budget lines merely to group costs. A package total is not a purchase order, cash outflow or actual spend.
2. Can need dates and lead times reliably determine an order deadline? Only within a stated calendar, sequencing model, duration definition, timestamp/timezone and evidence. A single countdown cannot express overlapping approvals, partial deliveries, holidays, supplier calendar and uncertainty. Calculate a proposed deadline, label assumptions and obtain planner review.
3. Can AI read drawings and directly create reliable model/estimate/schedule? Not established. Propose evidence-linked suggestions and qualified review, with explicit missing/conflict state and no automatic model, estimate, baseline, contract or safety action. Validate extraction by task, drawing type and risk, rather than adopt an arbitrary model confidence threshold.

## Evidence and boundaries

- The prior project plan says procurement totals share estimate lines, uses working-back lead times and requires separate AI review before edits [3]. The CP0–CP3 note proposes stable project-scoped identity, quantity provenance, scenario snapshots and cost allocation [1]; neither is proven application functionality. A GitHub code search in `src/js/engine` for both `RFI` and `procurement` returned no matches, but a search cannot establish absence of equivalent functionality under other names; a full source audit is still needed.
- The US DOE guide [4] discusses maintaining baseline and forecast schedules, traceable logic, long-lead procurements and configuration control. GAO [5] identifies reliable scheduling and risk analysis as relevant to understanding cost effects of delays. Both are useful public agency guidance, not a universal contractual rule or proof that a simple date calculation implements CPM or a risk model.
- A peer-reviewed construction symbol-detection study [6] tested 15 drawings split into 19,995 patches, observed class imbalance and false positives, and reported inconsistent performance by class; symbols overlapped, varied or resembled background shapes. These results do not measure our product or every drawing category; patch counts are not independent project counts. Its false positives demonstrate why high aggregate detection performance does not establish complete takeoff.
- An AWS/TwinKnowledge case study [7] describes difficulties using off-the-shelf models for drawing graphics and highlights links between plans, details and schedules; it is vendor-described and not independent comparative validation. NIST's generative AI risk profile [8] discusses confabulation, privacy and other risks. It is cross-sector guidance, not a product certification or specific acceptance threshold.

## CP4 proposed data and audit contract

A **package** has stable project-scoped ID, revision, scope, buyer/owner, status, originator and links to estimate-line IDs and allocation fractions or quantities. It is a grouping and decision record, not another priced copy. The linked estimate snapshot identifies currency, units, source and approvals; package aggregation must exclude incompatible currencies, uncertain or withdrawn lines and declare exclusions. Sum each line's allocations at full precision, require fractions between zero and one, reject >1, and report the difference as unallocated. A deliberately shared line needs recorded allocation basis and split history. Display adjustments, taxes, contingency and waste according to their source line and contract policy, never add them again just because a package exists. Baselines stay immutable; a working change creates a new approved version by the established governance.

Need-on-site is a demand from an activity or approved milestone, not a supplier promise. A package may have multiple demands, split deliveries or separate activities. Model separately: submittal preparation, review, release, fabrication, transport, inspection and site acceptance; each gets source, duration, units, calendar, dependencies, uncertainty and status as appropriate. The responsible planner decides which are sequential or overlapping. Lead time may be working days, calendar days or negotiated date; never silently convert. Specify time zone, cutoffs, holidays, half days and inclusion conventions, and handle cross-calendar interfaces. Missing duration or calendar produces unknown deadline, not zero. A deterministic date is a planning scenario, not a probabilistic certainty; risk analysis requires a defensible network and risk inputs.

Example deadline convention for the toy fixture: need by start of Friday 16 Jan 2026, five prior working days on a Mon–Fri calendar, Monday 12 Jan treated as a hypothetical holiday; do not count need day. Count 15, 14, 13, 9 and 8 Jan; deadline is Thu 8 Jan. This is not a verified actual holiday, supplier lead time, time-of-day rule, time-zone calculation, or contractual commitment. A human must verify whether ordering by start or end of day is actually sufficient. Partial deliveries, transit calendars and cutoff times are outside that example.

RFIs/issues link to source documents and relevant element, estimate line, activity and package IDs, with owner, question, dates, state, response and attachments. They flag possible impacts as suggestions or unresolved, not automatic revisions. A reviewed response is evidence, not automatically a change order; only authorized change control can update price, schedule or design. Quality records link inspection/checklist, scope, standard edition, reviewer, date, result and evidence; distinguish incomplete, failed, passed and not applicable. A checklist or model color is not a certified inspection or authorization for unsafe work. Do not send orders, notify suppliers, certify work or expose pricing without explicit authority.

## CP5 proposed evidence-first intake

Manual, offline core remains usable without AI. Upload documents only when the user has rights and consent; default to local processing or no processing until the provider, residency, retention, training use, encryption and contract are approved. Before any external upload show file identities and sensitivity, purpose, recipients and retention. Do not place private drawings or prompts into public PRs. Enforce access control, controlled deletion and audit retention rules; supplier/model version changes must trigger review and revalidation. A drawing can contain sensitive facility detail or embedded malicious instructions: treat all extracted text as untrusted content, never executable instructions, and prevent model output from directly calling tools, changing baselines, procuring, or accessing unrelated files.

Ingest immutable original with content hash, document ID, sheet ID, revision, page, timestamp, extraction method and access scope. Page-level version authority is not always determined by latest date: title blocks can be stale, addenda may govern across sheets and contract precedence is project-specific. Record unresolved competing revisions and omitted sheets; do not silently choose. For each candidate claim preserve source region, page/image dimensions and coordinate transform, original text/vector element where available, units, source view/scale, referenced detail/schedule and unresolved links. Explicitly distinguish OCR text, vector extraction, model inference, user edit and accepted value. Confidence is vendor-specific, may be uncalibrated and cannot prove correctness or completeness. High-confidence errors and omitted items must be audited.

Calibration: distinguish scale printed on title block from the scale of each view and from the pixel scale of a scan. Printed dimensions govern when verified under project policy; never manufacture a measurement from pixels unless the view is calibrated with reviewed known references and scaling/aspect distortions addressed. Require at least two independent dimension checks (proposed), plus units and reviewer; detect conflicting dimensions and ambiguous scale. Do not infer thickness, hidden connections, quantities, costs, methods, schedule dependencies, compliance or safety from a render alone. An extraction without valid source evidence remains a question, not a model fact.

Workflow: draft proposals → reviewer sees drawing crop, revision and linked details, candidate and rationale/uncertainty → accept, correct, reject or defer → preview a typed, bounded diff against current revision → authorized user applies in a single recoverable transaction after precondition recheck. Review decisions record person, time, input version and changed value; source revision or accepted-scope change marks downstream proposal stale. Bulk acceptance needs explicit policy and must not bypass independent checks. Unapproved output must not mutate original model, activities, estimate or baseline. Generated elements require source provenance and supported geometry/identity semantics; no invented stable IFC GUIDs or assumptions about imported IDs.

## Evaluation and acceptance plan, not performed

Create a rights-cleared, representative project-level holdout corpus with expert annotations independent of the person building the extractor: vector and scanned PDFs, multi-view sheets, addenda, discipline variation, bad scan/rotation, dense lines, rare symbol classes, partial/blank sets and negative controls. Keep all versions of one project in the same split to avoid leakage. Record sampling plan, excluded cases, adjudication disagreements and inter-rater reliability; stratify by class, format and source, report denominators and uncertainty. Evaluate detection precision/recall and one-to-one matching rules at specified overlap, sheet-level omissions and false scope, dimension/unit and scale accuracy, revision/relationship resolution, quantity and cost error at the project level, and reviewer correction/time burden versus a blinded manual baseline. Specify tolerances with qualified practitioners before deployment, with adverse high-impact errors separately tracked. A high overall average can conceal a rare critical miss; abstention and visible coverage matter. Never equate self-reported model confidence with calibrated probability.

Security tests: poisoned text/prompt injection, malformed/oversized documents, unsafe file parsing, unauthorized external transfer, stale/revoked sessions, audit leakage and retention deletion; perform a dedicated security/privacy review. Product tests: one estimate line split across packages, unsupported units, rounding residual, mixed currencies, missing activity, changed revision, concurrent edits, duplicate submissions, role permissions, undo/save/reopen and offline mode. Schedule tests: weekends, project and supplier holidays, differing time zones, daylight saving, cutoffs, partial deliveries, delays and false/unknown lead-time alerts. These are proposed acceptance fixtures, not results.

## Isolated toy experiment

Run `python3 docs/research/fixtures/cp4-cp5-fixture.py`: 5-workday illustrative deadline is 2026-01-08; one fictional 1000-unit cost line split 60/40 yields 600+400 and zero unallocated; a single 60% assignment leaves 400; 120% total is rejected. A toy confusion table of 8 true positives, 2 false positives and 2 false negatives gives precision and recall 0.8. The script uses only Python standard library, no app code, vendor or real project. It is a narrow arithmetic check, not actual procurement software, CPM, OCR, model evaluation, independent verification or professional approval.
