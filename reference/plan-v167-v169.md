# Plan: V167 to V169, then the interiors

The working plan for the next three phases, so each starts with its scope, its data, the code it
builds on, its patches, its tests and its open questions settled. Written 2026-10-17, while V166's
last runs went. Each phase still researches first and updates this as it goes.

## How a phase runs (what V164 to V166 taught)

| Step | What | V166 took |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Research | How practice does it, and the free, keyless sources, each tested from here for browser access (CORS) | a session |
| 2. Questions | The owner's decisions asked together at the end of research, before any code | one round |
| 3. Plan | Read the code it builds on; write the patch split, the fixture's cases and the variant list together | |
| 4. Patches | `a` data, `b` the analyses, `c` rows, findings, hooks and version; ASCII asserted | three patches |
| 5. Suite | The fixture, a Python reference of every method, the suite | 98 checks |
| 6. Falsify | The phase's variants, then every amended suite's script on the final build | 118 variants, 38 min; 351 more, 2.6 h |
| 7. Regression and records | The full run; STATUS, guide, catalogue, PIPELINE, the research note as built | 8 min |

Lessons to apply from the start:
- **Write the variants with the suite, not after.** Listing V166's variants before any ran found
  eight checks that could not fail. Each variant names the check that catches it.
- **Test data that tells rules apart:**
  - a value on the exact edge of a rule (25 mph is 40.2 km/h, in no row of whole km/h);
  - a distractor inside the wrong rule's reach and outside the right one's (V166's door, first
    placed on the 30 m line both rules shared);
  - data shaped as OpenStreetMap shapes it (a street split into two ways, a tag the app must drop).
- **The suite sees what the user sees.** The address search turns the street map on, so the light
  map's colours are drawn; empty the undo stack (50 steps) before counting steps.
- **Long runs in pieces.** A background job stops at 2 hours: split the falsifications (V166's
  went in three) or detach them and check in. Start the amended suites' runs as soon as the build is
  final, and write the records while they run.
- **Patches written as files with an ASCII assert.** The Write tool turns `\u` escapes into
  characters (it did in V165 and V166).
- **A server without CORS is named, not proxied.** Test it from here before planning on it
  (ESA WorldCover refused, 2026-10-17).

## V167 Site analysis IV: land

**Builds on:**
- the site context (V133, V157), already fetched within its radius:
  - buildings with their height and use (`o.context.use`, `height`);
  - land use areas (`kind 'landuse'`, `tags.landuse`), green areas, trees, water;
- zoning (V160): the council layer under the lot, from Data Layers (V134);
- V161's parks and walk times;
- V164's isovists;
- the diagram engine (V164, V165): lines, bands and marks.

No new request is needed for land use and buildings. Zoning needs one more: the council layer's
districts within the window, and its `drawingInfo` for their colours. Data layers do not read
symbology today.

**Four layers:**
1. **Land use:** OpenStreetMap's land use areas and the buildings' uses, classed by LBCS activity
   (a crosswalk table, in the research), coloured, with the mix around the site (each use's share,
   and an entropy index).
2. **Figure-ground:** the buildings filled, the open ground empty. Spacematrix's measures (GSI,
   FSI, OSR, L) for 400 m around the site and per 100 m cell, from footprints and heights (storeys
   from `building:levels` or the height at 3 m); the grain from the footprints' sizes.
3. **Green and trees:**
   - the canopy: mapped trees with their crowns (a default where none is mapped), woods, parks;
   - the 3-30-300 check: the trees seen from the lot (V164's isovist), the canopy's share of the
     neighbourhood, and the walk to the nearest public green space (V161's walks).
4. **Zoning around the site:** the council layer's districts in its own colours, the lot's district
   named; where no layer is loaded, it says so.

**Findings:** the land use mix; the density and its Spacematrix type; 3-30-300; zoning around.

**Open:**
- the land use colours: decided, LBCS's hues adjusted to pass the colour-blindness checks;
- the neighbourhood's radius for the 30% (default 500 m; Konijnendijk does not fix one);
- the smallest green space (0.5 ha, said);
- the crown when none is mapped;
- LBCS 6000 to 9000's RGB values, still to read: planning.org could not be reached from here.

**Tests:** a fixture of land use areas, buildings with uses and heights, trees, a park, and a
council layer's answer with its renderer; a reference of the crosswalk, GSI, FSI, OSR and L, the
canopy and the 3-30-300 measures.

## V168 Assets: people, bikes, cars and trees

**Builds on:**
- the Assets library (V124): built-ins made of boxes and cylinders (`A3D_ASSET_STARTER`, `bimAsmMesh`);
- placing a model as a solid;
- the context's trees (V157: a hexagonal trunk and a 20-face crown).

Solids already cast shadows in the sun studies (`bimShadowCasters`), so placed trees shade
without more work.

**Scope:** each model is data and a low-detail mesh, its dimensions in Properties, from a cited
standard where there is one:
- **people:** standing (adult, child), seated, a wheelchair user; height, eye height, shoulder
  width, walking speed;
- **bikes:** a bike and its stand spacing;
- **vehicles:** a car, a van, a bus; the design vehicle's size, the parking bay, the turning path;
- **trees:** broadleaf and conifer; height, crown spread, deciduous or evergreen. A deciduous
  tree casts no crown shadow while bare: its months by latitude, said.

The context's trees use the same tree model.

**Decided:** made in-house now; valdrada's credited models converted later, once the app can
import glTF. **Open:** which pieces.

**Tests:**
- each model's dimensions against its source;
- placing and moving a model;
- a broadleaf shading in summer and not in winter;
- a thousand trees drawn as instances, within a memory budget.

## V169 Simulation S1: traffic and pedestrians around the site

**Builds on:**
- V166's movement data: streets with lanes, speeds, one-way, parking by side; signals, stops and
  crossings; doors, car parks, stops and the paths to them;
- V161's walk network;
- V152's GPU table for instanced drawing;
- BENCHMARK (V155).

The app paints only on demand: there is no frame loop. Its one animation is the camera's tween.

**Design** (from `research-valdrada.md`):
- **cars:** IDM car following, with turns at junctions by turn ratios and lanes capped by width
  (the width tag joins the kept tags);
- **signals:** where mapped, on a fixed two-phase cycle, said;
- **pedestrians:** on the sidewalks, crossing on green, between the doors, the stops and the car
  parks;
- **the loop:**
  - a frame loop only while it runs, with a fixed simulation step of 0.2 s, drawn between steps;
  - the agents drawn alone over the last frame of the model where the renderer allows, so the
    model is not redrawn each frame;
- **results as layers:** the queue at the lot's entry, people at each door, delay at the crossings;
- **light by design:** see the note.

**Open:**
- **the lot's own trips: decided, from the design's parking.** Free trip-generation rates were not
  found (ITE's and TRICS's are sold), so each space makes an assumed number of peak-hour trips by
  usage, stated on the layer;
- **the demand:** volumes by street class at a chosen hour, from FHWA's typical daily traffic (as
  the noise analysis uses it), with a peak-hour share; assumed and said;
- **signal timing:** a cycle by the main street's class, from research;
- **the run:** its length, and the warm-up.

**Tests:**
- IDM against a Python reference: a leader and a follower settling to the equilibrium gap;
- no car entering a junction on red;
- every car spawned either present or gone;
- the walkers' crossings within the green;
- the page's memory settling within its budget over a long run;
- a frame within budget at Light.
