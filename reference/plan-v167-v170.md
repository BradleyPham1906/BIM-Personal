# Plan: V167 to V170, then the interiors

The working plan for the next four phases, so each starts with its scope, its data, the code it
builds on, its patches, its tests and its open questions settled. Written 2026-10-17, while V166's
last runs went. Each phase still researches first and updates this as it goes.

## How a phase runs (what V164 to V166 taught)

| Step | What | V166 took |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Research | To `research-protocol.md`: practice, the standard and its edition, places, data tested from here (CORS), implementations read as text, edges, the app's code; each fact's evidence level, nothing the code uses left unverified | a session |
| 2. Questions | The owner's decisions asked together at the end of research, before any code | one round |
| 3. Plan | Read the code it builds on; write the patch split, the fixture's cases and the variant list together | |
| 4. Patches | `a` data, `b` the analyses, `c` rows, findings, hooks and version; ASCII asserted | three patches |
| 5. Suite | The fixture, a Python reference of every method, the suite | 98 checks |
| 6. Falsify | The phase's variants, then every amended suite's script on the final build | 118 variants, 38 min; 351 more, 2.6 h |
| 7. Regression and records | The full run; STATUS, guide, catalogue, PIPELINE, the research note as built | 8 min |

Lessons to apply from the start:
- **Write the variants with the suite, not after.** Listing V166's variants before any ran found
  eight checks that could not fail. Each variant names the check that catches it.
- **Test data that tells rules apart:**
  - a value on the exact edge of a rule (25 mph is 40.2 km/h, in no row of whole km/h);
  - a distractor inside the wrong rule's reach and outside the right one's (V166's door, first
    placed on the 30 m line both rules shared);
  - data shaped as OpenStreetMap shapes it (a street split into two ways, a tag the app must drop).
- **The suite sees what the user sees.** The address search turns the street map on, so the light
  map's colours are drawn; empty the undo stack (50 steps) before counting steps.
- **Long runs in pieces.** A background job stops at 2 hours: split the falsifications (V166's
  went in three) or detach them and check in. Start the amended suites' runs as soon as the build is
  final, and write the records while they run.
- **Patches written as files with an ASCII assert.** The Write tool turns `\u` escapes into
  characters (it did in V165 and V166).
- **Never edit a suite while its falsification runs:** each variant runs the suite file as it is
  then, so a mid-run edit mixes two suites (V166 had to stop and re-run V155's).
- **A failure in the regression gets its suite's falsify script re-run too.** V166's regression
  failures in V155 and V159 led there to one check that could never fail since V156 and one race.
  Know which engine a helper draws with: V155's frames are WebGPU only with offscreen rendering.
- **A server without CORS is named, not proxied.** Test it from here before planning on it
  (ESA WorldCover refused, 2026-10-17).

## V167 Standards registry

The owner (2026-10-17): "building a system based on the industry standards (but flexible enough when
the standards changed)". The research and the design are `research-standards-registry.md`.

**Builds on:**
- about 90 constants in the site analysis modules, each a standard or an assumption with its citation
  in a comment beside it (BIM_MV_*, BIM_DG_ROADS, BIM_ACC_*, BIM_LAND_*, BIM_CLIM_*, BIM_ZN_*, BIM_CEN_*);
- V148's result layers, which already say Out of date when their model changes;
- the findings, which say their method and source in words.

**Scope:**
- a record per edition: publisher, edition, dates, where it applies, its source to the page, its
  parameters and tables in our own words, the method version that implements it, what it supersedes;
- one reader (`bimStd`), and a suite asking that no standard's value is left in code;
- profiles by place as a tree (international, country, region, city), the most specific edition
  winning, an international fallback (ISO, WHO) where a place has none, or said so where none exists;
- overrides per project with a reason, shown on every result that used them;
- provenance on every layer and finding, and Out of date when an edition, a profile or an override
  changes;
- Properties > Standards: each topic, the edition in force for the site, its values, the override;
- new editions with app versions and as standards pack files, their differences shown first;
- methods versioned when a revision changes their structure;
- checks shaped as IDS (applicability and requirements), for the interiors later;
- V164 to V166's constants moved into records. Their suites stay as they are: passing unchanged is
  what proves the move.

**The US standards (the owner asked, 2026-10-17: "AASHTO, PennDOT Pub, MUTCD, ... civil
engineering, architectural aviation standards?").** What the app holds today, read from its code:
- **in the app, as constants:** IBC Table 1004.5's occupant load factors (2018 and 2021) and IBC 202's
  gross floor area (V105); ASCE 7's strength combinations 1 and 2 for the frame (V125); AISC W and C
  shapes at nominal sizes (V126); AIA and NCS layer-name presets; FHWA's functional classes, FEMA's
  flood zones, the Census Bureau's ACS and LEED ND in the site analysis;
- **not in the app:** AASHTO (the LRFD Bridge Design Specifications, the Green Book), PennDOT's
  publications (Design Manuals 2 and 4, Publication 408, the Roadway Construction Standards), the MUTCD,
  FAA's advisory circulars, the ADA Standards, NFPA, ASHRAE, ACI 318; MasterFormat and Uniformat only as
  a free code field;
- **in the repository only:** `reference/penndot/`, PennDOT's bridge programs' input formats (the
  parked Track C), not its design standards.

V167's research catalogues them for the US profile, by the app's disciplines: architecture (IBC, the
ADA Standards and ICC A117.1, IECC and ASHRAE 90.1), civil (the Green Book, the MUTCD, PennDOT's Design
Manual 2 and its standards), bridges (AASHTO LRFD and PennDOT's Design Manual 4, which amends it),
structures (ASCE 7, ACI 318, AISC 360), aviation (FAA's Airport Design advisory circular). Each with
its current edition, publisher, where it is adopted, and whether it is free (federal works: the MUTCD,
FAA's circulars, the ADA Standards; PennDOT's own publications) or sold (AASHTO, ASCE, ACI; ICC's codes
free to read, not to copy). Sold texts are never copied: their values in our own words, cited, from a
copy the owner holds. Every edition is U until read at its source. On 2026-10-17 this environment
refused every one of their hosts (penndot.pa.gov, mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov, fhwa.dot.gov, faa.gov, ada.gov,
access-board.gov, codes.iccsafe.org, transportation.org, aisc.org, govinfo.gov, ecfr.gov), so the
catalogue waits on the network settings or on documents the owner provides outside the repository.

**Open, for its research:**
- the international fallback for each topic the app already applies (noise: ISO 9613-2 and WHO's
  2018 guidelines; walking speed; stress and PTAL, which have no international edition);
- the jurisdiction tree's source (the site's country and region from the address search);
- the pack file's format, and its signature, so a pack's origin is known.

**Researched to the source (2026-10-18; `research-standards-registry.md`, "Read at the source", and
`research-standards-inventory.md`):**
- **The record** takes OpenFisca's shape: a topic (`ptal`, `crtn`, `ibc.occupant_load`), its
  publisher and title, and its editions. An edition has its dates (published, in force from,
  superseded), where it applies (place keys), its status, its source (title, section or table, page)
  with the level it was read at, and its values: parameters (a value and a unit), scales (bands with
  thresholds) and indexed tables (by class), each with its reference. A value announced but not in
  force is `expected`. The method version that implements it is named.
- **The reader:**
  - `bimStd(topic, name)` returns the value in force for the project's place and date, or the
    project's override;
  - `bimStdEdition(topic)` returns the edition in force, or none with the reason;
  - `bimStdSay(topic, name)` returns the value as words, with its unit, for every sentence that
    states it;
  - `bimStdStamp(topics)` returns the provenance a result keeps (topic, edition, override, a hash of
    the values).
- **The place:** one Nominatim reverse request (`zoom=10`, `addressdetails=1`) when the site is placed
  gives `country_code`, `ISO3166-2-lvl4` and the city. They are kept with the site, shown in
  Properties > Site and changeable there. The path is international, then the country, then the
  region, then the city. The most specific edition wins; none at all is said. Offline, the owner
  picks.
- **Checks:** IDS 1 cannot check geometry, calculated values or anything outside the model (its own
  manual). Property and attribute requirements take IDS's shape and can be exported. Clearances,
  distances, slopes and areas stay the registry's own rules, with IDS's three outcomes. A future IDS
  checker is tested against buildingSMART's IFC and IDS test pairs, used unchanged and credited
  (CC BY-ND 4.0).
- **Values in words:** the build has 81 matches of phrases that state a standard's number, comments
  included (the inventory counts them by phrase).
  Each is built through `bimStdSay`, and the suite reads the strings as well as the code.
- **The order of the move:**
  1. The reader, the format and the place path, with V164 to V166's S records (TfL, CRTN, FHWA,
     WHO, LEED ND, FEMA), their suites unchanged.
  2. IBC's occupant loads and ASCE 7's combinations, V105's and V125's suites unchanged.
  3. The app's assumptions as overridable records.
  4. The slope bands' 8.33% stays marked U until its source is read.

**Tests:**
- every record against its source;
- a profile chosen by the site's place, a fallback, a topic with none;
- an override shown on its results;
- Out of date after an edition changes, and a re-run showing the difference;
- a pack's differences shown before it applies;
- a variant per record, so the suites read the registry, not a copy.

## V168 Site analysis IV: land

**Builds on:**
- the site context (V133, V157), already fetched within its radius:
  - buildings with their height and use (`o.context.use`, `height`);
  - land use areas (`kind 'landuse'`, `tags.landuse`), green areas, trees, water;
- zoning (V160): the council layer under the lot, from Data Layers (V134);
- V161's parks and walk times;
- V164's isovists;
- the diagram engine (V164, V165): lines, bands and marks.

No new request is needed for land use and buildings. Zoning needs one more: the council layer's
districts within the window, and its `drawingInfo` for their colours. Data layers do not read
symbology today.

**Four layers:**
1. **Land use:** OpenStreetMap's land use areas and the buildings' uses, classed by LBCS activity
   (a crosswalk table, in the research), coloured, with the mix around the site (each use's share,
   and an entropy index).
2. **Figure-ground:** the buildings filled, the open ground empty. Spacematrix's measures (GSI,
   FSI, OSR, L) for 400 m around the site and per 100 m cell, from footprints and heights (storeys
   from `building:levels` or the height at 3 m); the grain from the footprints' sizes.
3. **Green and trees:**
   - the canopy: mapped trees with their crowns (a default where none is mapped), woods, parks;
   - the 3-30-300 check: the trees seen from the lot (V164's isovist), the canopy's share of the
     neighbourhood, and the walk to the nearest public green space (V161's walks).
4. **Zoning around the site:** the council layer's districts in its own colours, the lot's district
   named; where no layer is loaded, it says so.

**Colour alone cannot carry it** (validated 2026-10-17): LBCS's families on the reference palette's
hues fail when any two classes may touch, as on a map. The worst is institutional blue against
industrial violet, normal-vision Delta E 10.5 on the light map and 9.8 on the dark plan, against a floor
of 15. Each class carries a second cue: a texture where colours are close, its short label on large
areas, the key, and Properties on a click; every area outlined (yellow is 1.89:1 on the light map).

**Findings:** the land use mix; the density and its Spacematrix type; 3-30-300; zoning around.

**Open:**
- the land use colours: decided, LBCS's hues adjusted to pass the colour-blindness checks;
- the neighbourhood's radius for the 30% (default 500 m; neither Konijnendijk nor Browning et al.
  fix one, read 2026-10-18);
- the smallest green space: 0.5 ha, public, reached by walking distance (Browning et al., 2024, an
  expert consensus, read in full 2026-10-18);
- the crown when none is mapped: decided, the context's own rule (V157: 0.6 of the height, 8 m where unmapped);
- LBCS's colours: read in APA's workbook on 2026-10-18 (6000 2F4F4F, 7000 90EE90, 8000 228B22,
  9000 FFFFFF), and the crosswalk checked against the activities' descriptions (the second round,
  below);
- the entropy index's N: eight families proposed, for the owner.

**Tests:** a fixture of land use areas, buildings with uses and heights, trees, a park, and a
council layer's answer with its renderer; a reference of the crosswalk, GSI, FSI, OSR and L, the
canopy and the 3-30-300 measures.

## V170 Simulation S1: traffic and pedestrians around the site

**Builds on:**
- V166's movement data: streets with lanes, speeds, one-way, parking by side; signals, stops and
  crossings; doors, car parks, stops and the paths to them;
- V161's walk network;
- V152's GPU table for instanced drawing;
- BENCHMARK (V155).

The app paints only on demand: there is no frame loop. Its one animation is the camera's tween.

**Design** (from `research-valdrada.md`):
- **cars:** IDM car following, with turns at junctions by turn ratios and lanes capped by width
  (the width tag joins the kept tags);
- **signals:** where mapped, on a fixed two-phase cycle, said;
- **pedestrians:** on the sidewalks, crossing on green, between the doors, the stops and the car
  parks;
- **the loop:**
  - a frame loop only while it runs, with a fixed simulation step of 0.2 s, drawn between steps;
  - the agents drawn alone over the last frame of the model where the renderer allows, so the
    model is not redrawn each frame;
- **results as layers:** the queue at the lot's entry, people at each door, delay at the crossings;
- **light by design:** see the note.

**Open:**
- **the lot's own trips: decided, from the design's parking.** Free trip-generation rates were not
  found (ITE's and TRICS's are sold), so each space makes an assumed number of peak-hour trips by
  usage, stated on the layer;
- **the demand:** volumes by street class at a chosen hour, from FHWA's typical daily traffic (as
  the noise analysis uses it) times a peak-hour share K. Agencies define K as the 30th-highest
  hour of the year over the daily traffic (DHV = K x AADT); Delaware's table runs from 9.3% to
  14.3% by traffic pattern. Proposed 10%, assumed and said
  (https://deldot.gov/Publications/manuals/traffic_counts/pdfs/2024/Growth and K&D Factors.pdf);
- **signal timing, derived, not picked:** each crossing's minimum green is the MUTCD's 7 s WALK
  plus its clearance, the crossing's length at 3.5 ft/s (1.07 m/s), then amber and all-red; two
  phases make the cycle, held between 60 and 120 s (the range to confirm in FHWA's Signal Timing
  Manual) (https://www.nymtc.org/portals/0/pdf/Transportation%20Safety/NYSAMPO%20TrafficSignalTimingFactSheet_2012.pdf);
- **trips per parking space:** no free table exists (ITE's Parking Generation is sold). Local
  turnover studies give the shape: a retail lot turned over 3.2 times per space over a 9-hour
  weekday (Bar Harbor), offices fuller with less turnover (Wilsonville). The rates by usage are
  assumed, said on the layer, and set once in the simulation's settings;
- **the run:** its length, and the warm-up.

**Tests:**
- IDM against a Python reference: a leader and a follower settling to the equilibrium gap;
- no car entering a junction on red;
- every car spawned either present or gone;
- the walkers' crossings within the green;
- the page's memory settling within its budget over a long run;
- a frame within budget at Light.

## Appendix: V168 (land) research so far

Working notes, to become section 6 of `research-site-analysis-in-model.md` when V168 starts.

- LBCS colours: one colour per top-level category, all subcategories the same, or shades of it.
  APA recommends specifying RGB, not names. Activity: 1000 residential yellow 255,255,0; 2000
  shopping, business, trade red 255,0,0; 3000 industrial purple 160,32,240; 4000 social,
  institutional, infrastructure blue 0,0,255; 5000 travel or movement grey 190,190,190; 6000 mass
  assembly dark slate grey (value to read); 7000 leisure light green; 8000 natural resources forest
  green; 9000 not classified. Copies disagree on hex (UWSP's Appendix C has wrong hex); the RGB
  triplets agree. planning.org and the Santa Fe copy unreachable from here (DNS refused).
  Sources: https://archives.santafecountynm.gov/media/files/Planning/San%20Marcos/Land%20Based%20Classification%20System.pdf,
  https://www3.uwsp.edu/cnr-ap/clue/Documents/Comprehensive_Planning/Land_Use_Resource_Guide_Appendix_C.pdf,
  https://planning.org/lbcs/standards
  NOTE: pure LBCS colours (FF0000, 0000FF...) fail CVD/contrast checks; they would need the
  validator, or a muted variant keyed to the same hues, said in the key.
- Zoning colours: no national standard. Yellow residential is the one dependable convention;
  commercial orange/red, industrial purple/grey vary (MSU Extension; SF Planning; Austin). Darker =
  more intense ("colour ramping"); blue institutional, green parks/agriculture. The map's legend is
  the authority: draw a council layer in its own district colours where it gives them.
  Sources: https://www.canr.msu.edu/uploads/resources/pdfs/mapcolorsforlanduse.pdf,
  https://sfplanning.org/resource/zoning-use-districts, https://austintexas.gov/sites/default/files/files/Planning/zoning_landuse_chart.pdf
- Spacematrix (Berghauser Pont and Haupt, 2010): FSI (floor area / area), GSI (footprint / area),
  OSR ((1 - GSI) / FSI), L (FSI / GSI, mean storeys); types cluster in the FSI-GSI plot. Context
  massing already has OSM footprints and heights. Check formulas against the book.
  Sources: https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_0b2ef4f4-d21e-42cd-a515-8e3da4b030ae,
  https://journal-buildingscities.org/articles/83
- Grain: momepy's morphological tessellation (Voronoi from footprints), coverage area ratio per
  cell (BSD-3; ideas only). https://github.com/pysal/momepy
- 3-30-300 (Konijnendijk, J. Forestry Research 2023): 3 trees seen from every home, school,
  workplace; at least 30% canopy in every neighbourhood; at most 300 m to the nearest public green
  space. A rule of thumb; "green space" not defined precisely (some say 0.5-1 ha).
  https://journal.hep.com.cn/jfr/EN/10.1007/s11676-022-01523-z
- Canopy data: OSM natural=tree/tree_row/wood, landuse=forest (patchy). ESA WorldCover 10 m (2020,
  2021), CC BY 4.0, COGs in s3 esa-worldcover, 3x3 degree tiles, tree cover a class; browser access
  (CORS) not confirmed -- to test.
- OSM landuse: residential, commercial (services, offices), retail (shops), industrial, education,
  institutional (ambiguous), construction, etc. No standard OSM-to-LBCS crosswalk found.
  https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:landuse
- Tested 2026-10-17: ESA WorldCover's bucket (esa-worldcover.s3.eu-central-1.amazonaws.com) answers a
  range read (206) but sends no Access-Control-Allow-Origin, and the CORS preflight is refused (403):
  a page cannot read it. Named, not proxied -- canopy from OSM's trees, woods and parks, or a file.

## 2026-10-17, second pass (while V166's regression ran)

- 3-30-300 measured: Browning et al. (2024), "Measuring the 3-30-300 rule to help cities meet nature
  access thresholds", Science of the Total Environment: an expert consensus. The 3 from surveys and
  window-view analyses; the 30 from high-resolution land cover; the 300 by network analysis (walk
  distance, not the crow's). The green space: public, at least 0.5 ha (as a summary of the consensus
  puts it). Applied: Olomouc (canopy 22% at most), Nagpur, Ferrara (a composite index).
  https://repository.essex.ac.uk/41892/ ; https://dspace.library.uu.nl/bitstream/handle/1874/436762/1-s2.0-S0048969723063660-main.pdf
  For the app: the 3 by V164's isovist from the lot (trees whose crown it sees), the 30 from the
  context's trees, woods and parks within the neighbourhood, the 300 by V161's walk times to a
  public green space of 0.5 ha or more.
- The crown where none is mapped: the context already sets one (V157): 0.6 of the tree's height, the
  height 8 m where unmapped (BIM_CTX_TREE_H). V168 reads o.context.crown, one rule.
- LBCS activity colours: 1000 yellow 255,255,0; 2000 red 255,0,0; 3000 purple 160,32,240; 4000 blue
  0,0,255; 5000 grey 190,190,190; 6000 dark slate grey (47,79,79 by its name; the table's own value
  was cut off). 7000 to 9000 not read: planning.org and UWSP refuse from here (DNS). The Site
  dimension's 1000, natural state, is light green #90EE90 (corrected 2026-10-18: activity 7000 is
  light green #90EE90 too; see the second round below).
- Council colours: an ArcGIS layer's JSON (?f=json) carries drawingInfo.renderer; a uniqueValue
  renderer has its field and uniqueValueInfos, each a value, a label and a symbol (esriSFS, color
  [r,g,b,a] 0-255, an outline esriSLS); defaultSymbol for the rest. V134 does not read it yet.
  https://developers.arcgis.com/javascript/3/jsapi/uniquevaluerenderer.html
- THE PALETTE (validator, every pair, as a map needs): LBCS's families on the reference palette's
  documented hues -- residential yellow #eda100/#c98500, commercial red #e34948/#e66767, industrial
  violet #6250d6/#9085e9, institutional blue #2a78d6/#3987e5, leisure and natural green #008300 (light
  map, surface #f2efe9 / dark plan, surface #1d2024). Every pair: FAIL, the worst institutional
  blue against industrial violet, normal-vision Delta E 10.5 light and 9.8 dark (floor 15), CVD 4.5
  and 1.9. Magenta for industrial instead fails against red (13.2). As the method says, no set of
  four or more hues passes when any two may touch: colour alone cannot carry a land use map.
  So each class carries a second cue: a fixed texture where colours are close (industrial hatched
  45 degrees, assembly 135, natural resources dotted), the class's short label on areas large
  enough, the key, and Properties on a click. Yellow (1.89:1) and aqua sit below 3:1 on the light
  map: outlines on every area.
- A first crosswalk, OpenStreetMap to LBCS activity (to confirm against the standard's descriptions):
  1000 residential: landuse=residential; building=house, apartments, residential, detached, terrace,
  dormitory. 2000 shopping, business, trade: landuse=commercial, retail; shop=*, office=*; building=
  commercial, retail, office; amenity=restaurant, cafe, bank, fast_food, pub. 3000 industrial:
  landuse=industrial; building=industrial, warehouse; man_made=works. 4000 institutional and
  infrastructure: landuse=education, institutional, civic_admin; amenity=school, university,
  college, hospital, clinic, library, townhall, police, fire_station, courthouse, post_office;
  power=substation. 5000 travel or movement: amenity=parking; landuse=railway; aeroway=*; highway
  areas. 6000 mass assembly: leisure=stadium; amenity=theatre, cinema, place_of_worship (to check:
  LBCS may place worship elsewhere). 7000 leisure: leisure=park, playground, pitch, sports_centre,
  golf_course; landuse=recreation_ground. 8000 natural resources: landuse=farmland, forest, orchard,
  vineyard, quarry, meadow. 9000 no human activity or unclassified: natural=wood, scrub, grassland

**The land use tags.** iD has presets for 28 values of `landuse`:
- residential;

- education, religious, cemetery, military, railway, garages;

**Trees and parks.** A tree may carry `height`, `circumference` and `diameter_crown`. A park may carry
`access`: the 300 m test's "public" green space is a park whose `access` is not `private` or `no`.
The context does not read a park's `access` yet: V168 adds it.

**Council colours (Esri's own reading):**
- A unique value renderer keys on `field1`, with `field2` and `field3` joined by `fieldDelimiter`
  when given.
- Each of `uniqueValueInfos` has a `value`, a `label` and a `symbol`; `defaultSymbol` takes the rest.
- Values compare loosely, because a numeric field may come as a string.
- A fill (`esriSFS`) has `color` as `[r, g, b, a]` with alpha out of 255.
- Its `outline` is a line symbol (`esriSLS`); `esriSLSNull` draws none.
- Only solid fills are drawn; hatched styles are left out.
- `drawingInfo.transparency` is a percentage (opacity is `1 - t/100`).
- Symbol widths are points (1.333 px each).

**Floor area and storeys.** momepy takes floors as `floor(height / floor_height)`, 3 m by default, so a
building under 3 m tall has none. V168 uses `building:levels` where mapped, else that rule with at
least one storey, and its suite has a 2.5 m building.

**The mix.** Shannon's index `H = -sum(p_i ln p_i)`, as momepy computes it (over counts of objects).
For land use, `p_i` is each class's share of the area. The normalised entropy index (H divided by the
log of the number of classes) is in the planning literature, which is not reachable here: U, and
the number of classes it divides by is to be fixed (the nine LBCS families, or those present).

**3-30-300's green space.** The expert consensus summarised in the first notes gives 0.5 ha; Tygron's
3-30-300 demonstration uses 1 ha (from a search summary: U). With V167, the smallest green space is
a registry parameter, 0.5 ha by default, overridable with a reason.

**Density (Spacematrix):**
- Berghauser Pont: FSI, GSI and the network density N are the main measures. OSR (spaciousness)
  and L (the average number of floors) are derived from them, and so is W (the block's size).
- Buildings and Cities:
  - FSI is the total floor area divided by the land area, and can be any positive value. Its other
    names are plot ratio and FAR.
  - GSI is the ground floor area divided by the land area, up to 1, where 1 means the buildings
    cover the land.
  - FSI divided by GSI is the average height in storeys.
- OSR's formula, `(1 - GSI) / FSI`, is still from papers citing the book (S): the paper read here
  names OSR but does not write it out.
- V168 uses the footprint as the ground floor area: context massing has nothing else.

**3-30-300, as Browning et al. wrote it:**
- **The rule:**
  - everyone sees at least 3 well-established trees from their home, workplace or place of
    learning (mature, larger trees, after Konijnendijk 2023);
  - at least 30% tree canopy cover in their neighbourhood;
  - a high-quality public green space of at least 0.5 ha within 300 m. The text cites
    Konijnendijk 2022 for this, and the figure caption cites 2021.
- **The neighbourhood is not fixed.** The studies use either administrative zones (census tracts)
  or areas around the person's location. No radius is given, so V168's 500 m is a choice, said
  with the finding and a registry parameter.
- **The distance:**
  - The crow's distance is simple and fast, but wrong across barriers (a road with no crossing).
  - A network distance sums the street links from the home to the green space's access points
    (its entrances, or its centroid).
  - The experts rated green space maps with network analysis best for the 300. V161's walk network
    is that; V168 measures to the park's nearest entrance or edge node.
- **The other two, as the experts rated them:**
  - Window-view analyses are best for the 3. The lot's isovist (V164) stands in for one, and the
    finding says so.
  - High-resolution land cover maps are best for the 30. OpenStreetMap's trees, woods and parks are
    weaker, since WorldCover cannot be read from a page. The finding says the canopy is from
    mapped trees.

**The mix's index.**
- Cervero's conventional Shannon entropy (as Chen and Song quote it) is `-sum(A_i ln A_i) / ln N`.
  `A_i` is a use's share, and `N` is "the total number of represented land use types". It is 1 for
  an even balance and 0 for one use.
- Its known flaws:
  - With `N` counting only the uses present, two uses at half each score the same as nine at a
    ninth each (the "equal composition" problem).
  - The result depends on the area drawn (the modifiable areal unit problem).
  - Im and Choi (2018), as they report, found a less even mix did better for pedestrian volume.
- V168 proposes `N` fixed at the eight families 1000 to 8000 (9000 is left out of the mix), so
  places compare. The shares are by area, and the index is said with its `N` and radius. This is
  for the owner to decide at the end of research.
